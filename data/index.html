VVF 3.0 · Answering the note on screen 04

What we can actually tell a creator about their own account.

Before screen 04 gets designed, here is every signal we could put on it, where each one comes from, what it costs to get, and whether it changes what they film tomorrow. The screen gets built from the top of this list, not from whatever looks good in a mockup.

01

Three places the data can come from

They are not equally cheap and they are not equally useful, and the most useful one is the one we cannot have on day one. That tension is the whole design problem on screen 04.

Source A

Public profile and post metadata

The video list and everything attached to it: views, likes, comments, shares, saves, duration, caption, hashtags, sound, cover image, timestamp. No login. Just the handle.

Cost: near zero · Latency: under a second
Source B

The video files themselves

What is actually said and shown. Transcript with timestamps, the first frame, on-screen text, cut rate, whether there is music under the voice. Still no login, but we have to fetch and process each video.

Cost: real · Latency: seconds per video
Source C

TikTok account connect

The numbers only the owner can see. Retention, watch time, where the views came from, how many followers each video won. This is the half that actually explains a flop.

Cost: an OAuth screen · Latency: instant once granted
02

Tier 1 · build screen 04 on these

Six signals. Each one is retrievable without a login, each one names something the creator can change in their next video, and between them they cover all five answers to “what’s your biggest problem?” from screen 02.

The reach diagnostic

answers “I can’t get views”
Reach ceilingSource AInstant

What it says on screenYour videos average 340 views on 412 followers. You are being shown to your followers and almost nobody else.

Median views divided by follower count. Under roughly 1× means TikTok is not pushing them past their own audience, which is a completely different problem from “my content is bad” and it is the one most small creators misdiagnose. This is the line that reframes the whole thing, and it costs one division.

Share rate against the nicheSource AInstant

What it says on screenPeople like your videos. They don’t send them to anyone. Your share rate is 0.2%. The breakouts we found in skincare run 1.4%.

Shares are the closest public proxy for the thing the algorithm actually rewards, and they are the one engagement number small creators never look at. Needs a per-niche benchmark computed from our own catalog, which we have. Highest-value signal available without a login.

The hook diagnostic

answers “my views die in the first seconds”
Time to the pointSource B~5s per video

What it says on screenYour hooks take 4.1 seconds to reach the point. The breakouts land it in 1.8.

Transcript with word-level timestamps, then find the first sentence that states a claim, problem or promise. Ivan named this one and he is right that it is the sharpest. It is also the first signal on this list that costs money to compute, because it needs the audio.

Opens on the product or opens on the problemSource B~2s per video

What it says on screen9 of your last 12 videos open on your product. Every breakout we found opens on the problem.

First frame plus the first three seconds of transcript, classified into a small fixed set: product, face, problem statement, result, text card, b-roll. One vision call on one frame. The most quotable line on the screen and the cheapest of the Source B signals, because it does not need the whole video.

The self-comparison

answers “I run out of ideas” and “I can’t stay consistent”
Your own breakout, and what was different about itSource A+ Source B

What it says on screenYour best video did 3.2× your median. It was the only one you opened by talking, and the only one with no music.

Find the breakout in their own feed, then diff its Source B attributes against the other eleven. This is the single most persuasive thing on the screen because it is not our opinion, it is their own evidence handed back to them. It also fails gracefully: if nothing stands out, we say the feed is flat and that flatness is the finding.

Cadence and gapsSource AInstant

What it says on screenYou posted 11 times in March and 3 times in April. Your April videos did worse, and that order is not a coincidence.

Posts per week and the longest gap. Weak on its own, strong when someone has picked “I can’t stay consistent” on screen 02, which is why screen 04 should choose its third card based on their answer there rather than showing the same three cards to everyone.

03

Tier 2 · cheap, real, not headline material

Worth computing because they cost almost nothing once the pipeline exists, and worth holding back because a screen with nine findings on it lands weaker than a screen with three.

On-screen text in the first second

Most feeds are watched muted. If nothing is written on frame one, the hook only exists for people with sound on.

Cut rate in the first five seconds

Frame differencing, no model needed. Separates “one long static take” from “four cuts before the sentence finishes”.

Duration against performance

Do their longer or shorter videos win. Needs more than twelve posts before it means anything.

Original sound or trending sound

Cheap, and it matters more in some niches than others. Reads as a tip, not a diagnosis.

Does it end on a call to action

Last three seconds of transcript. Speaks directly to “I can’t turn views into follows” and “I can’t get sales”.

Topic scatter

How many distinct subjects across their last twelve. High scatter is a real reason an account never builds an audience.

Words per minute in the opening

Free once the transcript exists. Pace is a genuine retention lever and nobody ever tells creators their own number.

Save rate

The intent proxy. Matters most to the people who answered “sell a product” on screen 02.

Loopability

First frame against last frame. Low diagnostic value, high delight value. A detail nobody expects us to have looked at.

04

Tier 3 · the reason TikTok connect stays on the table

Ivan’s call to lead with handle and email is right, and this is the list that makes the optional connect worth offering later rather than dropping. These five cannot be scraped, inferred or approximated. They are the numbers that actually explain a flop, and only the account owner can hand them over.

Retention curve and average watch timeNeeds connect

What it says on screenHalf your viewers leave at 2.4 seconds. On the breakouts in your niche, half are still there at 7.

The single most explanatory number in short form, and it is invisible from outside. Every Source B signal we compute is an attempt to guess at this number from the outside. Getting it directly turns inference into fact.

Traffic source split

For You against Following against Search against Profile. Confirms or kills the reach-ceiling diagnosis outright instead of implying it.

New followers per video

The literal answer to “I can’t turn views into follows”, which is one of the five problems we ask about on screen 02.

Audience active hours

Turns posting-time advice from a guess into their actual audience. Cheap advice, and it feels personal.

Impressions against views

Whether the video was served and skipped, or never served at all. Two completely different fixes.

The upsell writes itself, and it is not “connect for more features”.

Everything on screen 04 without the connect is an educated guess about why people stopped watching. With the connect, we can show them the exact second they stopped. That is a specific, provable thing to offer and it belongs at the moment they first disagree with a finding, not on the sign-in screen where it currently sits.

Which also means the connect should be offered after screen 04 has already been useful, framed as “we guessed at this. Want the real number?”

05

What this does to the screen

Twenty seconds is not true yet

Source A is instant. Source B is a fetch, a transcript and a vision call per video. Twelve videos is not a twenty-second job unless it runs in parallel and gets cached.

So the screen reveals in two waves

Paint the Source A findings immediately, then stream the hook findings in as they land. The wait stops being a spinner and starts being visible work.

Six videos, not twelve

Twelve is a nice number in copy but it triples the bill for very little extra signal. Six recent videos give the same three findings.

Three cards, and the third one is chosen

Card one is reach, card two is the hook, card three is picked from whatever they told us their biggest problem was on screen 02. Same structure for everyone, different third card.

Every line has to imply a change

If a finding does not tell them what to do differently in the next video, it is a stat, not an insight, and it belongs in Tier 2 where nobody sees it.

Benchmarks come from our own catalog

Every “you 4.1s, breakouts 1.8s” line needs a per-niche number. That is a real dependency on the catalog being deep enough in each niche before launch.

06

Open, and blocking

Private, new or empty accounts

Under five public videos and screen 04 has nothing to say. This is not an edge case at our exact audience, and it needs a designed fallback rather than an error state.

Cost per signup

Source B runs before anyone has paid us a cent. Needs a ceiling per account and a decision on whether we run it for every signup or only on request.

Fetching at volume

Reading public profiles for one creator is fine. Doing it for every signup on a growth spike is a different conversation, and it wants an answer before launch, not after.

Niche depth in the catalog

Benchmarks only exist where the catalog is deep. Which niches are actually deep enough today decides which niches we can let people search on screen 01.
